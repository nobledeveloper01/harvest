# ADR-0009 — The clips ship as AAC, and the gate learns to read it

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-06

## Context

Speech is the interface. ADR-0001 established that every prompt is a bundled
recording, because system text-to-speech has no voice for Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo or
Nigerian Pidgin. That decision is not in question here; what it costs is.

725 clips shipped as 8 kHz WAV came to **42 MB**, and those are placeholders
generated by `say`. Real native-speaker recordings at a usable sample rate would
be several times that, on a product whose design floor is a ₦40,000 handset and
a metered connection. An app that a farmer cannot afford to download is an app
that does not reach them, and no amount of care about the 5" screen fixes it.

WAV was the original choice for a specific reason, recorded as R5 at the time:
`audio-check` opens each clip with Python's `wave` module and proves it is not
empty. Compression breaks that, and **a gate that cannot read the file it is
gating is a gate that only checks the filesystem.** So the format change was
always going to be a gate rather than a chore.

## Decision

**AAC-LC, mono, 16 kHz, 32 kbps, in an `.m4a` container.**

Opus is the better codec for speech at low bitrates and it is not available:
Android decodes it natively, **iOS does not**, and `audioplayers` on iOS is
`AVAudioPlayer`. A format that works on one of two platforms is not a format.
AAC-LC is decoded natively by both, has been since the platforms existed, and
at 32 kbps mono is comfortable for a recorded voice.

**And `audio-check` parses the container rather than decoding it.** It walks the
MP4 atoms for `mdhd` (timescale and duration) and `stbl/stsz` (the size of every
encoded sample), and asserts two things: the clip is longer than a quarter of a
second, and its encoded payload exceeds **800 bytes per second**.

No decoder, deliberately. A gate that needs a codec installed is a gate that
stops running on the machine that does not have one, and this repository's rule
is that a gate that has been switched off is worse than one that was never
written.

## Consequences

- **The floor was measured, not chosen.** At these settings, three seconds of
  digital silence encodes to 196 bytes of payload — **62 bytes per second** —
  and three seconds of speech to 12,057, or **3,694**. The floor sits at 800,
  with roughly sixty times the margin below it and four times above. A real
  recording of a quiet voice has room; an empty one does not.
- **The rate is a ceiling as well as a floor now.** The bitrate this ADR fixes
  had nothing enforcing it: any clip large enough to contain a signal passed. The
  moment that matters is R1 — real recordings arrive from four native speakers,
  somebody converts them, and one forgotten `afconvert` flag ships the set at 64
  kbps or at 44.1 kHz stereo. Both were tried against the gate; both come out at
  2.1× and are named. The ceiling is 7,000 bytes per second, 1.75× nominal,
  chosen from the measured spread of the 920 clips here — 3,750 to 4,497, the
  variance being container overhead on short clips — so it is 56% clear of the
  heaviest real clip and still catches the next rate up. **Sixteen megabytes of
  speech becoming sixty-four is not audible to anybody; it is only visible to a
  farmer paying for the download.**
- **The parser was checked against something that did not share its
  assumptions.** Reading the container two independent ways — `mdhd`'s duration,
  and counting AAC frames out of `stsz` at 1024 samples each — agrees to the
  microsecond across twenty-five clips. Both differ from `afinfo` by a constant
  ~130 ms, which is AAC's priming: the container stores media duration and a
  player discards the priming samples. That the two parses agree is the
  evidence; matching a third tool that is measuring a different quantity would
  not have been.
- **What the check proves is narrower than it looks, and it says so.** It
  proves the clip contains an encoded signal of some size over some duration. It
  cannot tell speech from a fan, and does not pretend to — that is R1, and R1
  needs a person who speaks the language. What it *can* do is exactly what the
  `wave` check did, through a format the `wave` module cannot open, which is
  what R5 asked for.
- **Unreadable is still worse than missing**, and still treated that way: a file
  that is not a parseable MP4 with an audio track fails the same as an empty
  one, because it looks present to anything that only checks the filesystem.
- **`speech-budget.py` reads durations the same way**, so the report that will
  become R8 survived the change too rather than quietly measuring nothing.
- The format change was made while **every clip is still a placeholder**. That
  is the point of the timing: if the decision is wrong, nothing of value has
  been re-encoded, and R1's recordings arrive into a pipeline that already
  handles the real format rather than one that has to be rebuilt around them.
